Research Study · Language & Cognition

Inner Speech & Set-Shifting in Bilinguals

A study on how the language of internal thought relates to cognitive flexibility.

Estimated time: 18–22 minutes  ·  Desktop/laptop required  ·  Keyboard responses

Purpose

This study investigates whether the language bilingual individuals use in their inner speech is associated with their ability to shift efficiently between cognitive tasks — a component of executive function called set-shifting (Miyake et al., 2000).

What you will do

  • Complete a short vocabulary screening to verify English proficiency
  • Answer questions about your language background and inner speech habits
  • Brief mood check (4 items)
  • Perform a cued number-classification task measuring set-shifting

Eligibility

  • Aged 18 or above
  • Fluent in English at C1 level or above
  • A speaker of at least one additional language
  • Using a desktop or laptop with a physical keyboard (task requires F and J keys)

Your rights as a participant

Voluntary participation. Your participation is entirely voluntary. You may withdraw at any time during the study, for any reason, without penalty — simply close the browser tab. Any data collected up to that point will be discarded.

Anonymity. No names, email addresses, or identifying personal information are collected. You will be assigned a random participant ID generated at the moment you begin. If you came from Prolific, your Prolific ID is a pseudonymous identifier used only for compensation purposes and is not linked to your real identity.

Data storage and use. Your anonymised responses are submitted automatically and securely to the researcher's database at the end of the study — no file is saved on your own device. Data will be used only for academic research purposes, including potential publication in scientific journals, and will be retained for a maximum of 10 years, after which it will be permanently deleted.

Pre-registration. This study has been pre-registered on the Open Science Framework (OSF) before data collection began. The pre-registration is publicly available and contains the full study protocol and hypotheses.

Risks & support

This study involves answering questionnaires and performing a brief number-classification task. It poses minimal risk to participants. The task is not designed to assess intelligence or general ability — performance varies widely and is influenced by fatigue, time of day, and familiarity with computer tasks.

If you experience any distress during or after participation, please contact a mental health professional or a support helpline in your country (e.g. in the US: 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline; in the UK: Samaritans 116 123; international directory: findahelpline.com).

What this study was about

This study examined whether the language bilingual individuals use in inner speech is associated with set-shifting ability — the capacity to efficiently redirect attention between different cognitive tasks, a specific component of executive function identified by Miyake et al. (2000).

Your performance on the number classification task provided a measure of set-shifting, specifically your switch cost — the reaction time penalty incurred when switching from one classification rule to another. Your inner speech questionnaire responses will be correlated with switch costs across all participants.

The task included a cue-stimulus interval (CSI) manipulation — trials with short (200ms) and long (1200ms) preparation times — to examine whether preparation time modulates switch costs differently depending on inner speech habits.

Your Performance

CSI Preparation Effects

A larger reduction in switch cost from Short to Long CSI indicates greater ability to use preparation time to reconfigure task-sets — a theoretically meaningful individual difference.
